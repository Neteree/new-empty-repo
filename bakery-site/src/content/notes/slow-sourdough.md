---
title: Why our sourdough takes 36 hours
date: 2026-09-10
excerpt: Most of the work happens while we sleep. A long, cold rise gives a deeper flavour and a loaf that keeps for days.
art: loaf
---

We mix the dough on Thursday afternoon, and it doesn't see the oven until Saturday at 4am.

**Day one: the mix.** Flour, water, salt and our starter, which has been going since the bakery opened. The dough rests for a few hours at room temperature, with a fold every half hour.

**Overnight: the cold rise.** Shaped loaves go into the chiller for more than a day. The cold slows the yeast down while the bacteria keep working, and that's where the gentle sourness and the crackly, blistered crust come from.

**Saturday: the bake.** Straight from the chiller into a very hot oven. A long, cold rise also makes a loaf that stays good for three or four days, so one loaf really can last the weekend.
